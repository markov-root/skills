# Meta-document content standards

> **Purpose:** State the content-quality standard for the two meta-roles (index and template) — the
> telos each serves, the properties that make its content good, the test that discriminates a good
> map or authoring aid from a well-formed empty one, and a compact exemplar/anti-example for each.
>
> **Read this when:** authoring or reviewing an index of a record population, or a template meant to
> transfer a role's content judgment into the next authoring act.

The meta-roles exist to make other documents work: an index routes a reader to current authority;
a template transfers a role's content judgment without manufacturing a gate. Their distinct failure
is a form that looks complete but does not actually reduce a reader's cognitive load or surface the
author's hardest decisions. Read this alongside
[`documentation-structure.md`](documentation-structure.md) for the lifecycle and routing shape.

## The shared floor

A meta-document has two independent axes:

- **Well-formedness** — does it satisfy its structural contract (role, metadata, generation
  provenance for an index, placeholder discipline for a template)?
- **Warrant and content quality** — does the index route a scoped reader to current authority
  without silent omissions, and does the template surface the role's real content decisions?

Both matter, and neither substitutes for the other. **Structure hard, warrant loud**: generated
completeness (an index covering its registry) is not proof the map is useful, and a well-formed
template with inert placeholders does not elicit the role's substance.

## Index

**Telos:** Provide a complete, current map of a scoped record population with useful routing and
authority cues.

**Content-quality properties:**
- Generation provenance recorded; links resolve; coverage matches registry allocation.
- Grouping and summaries reduce cognitive load, not just list entries.
- Gaps and authority/currentness represented honestly rather than hidden by generated completeness.

**Discriminating test:** Does it route a scoped reader to current authority and expose the
territory's boundaries without silent omissions?

**Exemplar:** An index whose links resolve to the current records, whose grouping and one-line
summaries let a reader pick the right artifact, and whose deliberate gaps and dated boundaries are
stated rather than silently absent.

**Anti-example:** A generated index that looks complete because it lists every record, but whose
grouping is inert, whose summaries say nothing useful, and whose gaps and currentness are hidden —
so a reader routes to the wrong or stale authority.

## Template

**Telos:** Transfer a role's content judgment into the next authoring act without manufacturing
boilerplate or a creation-time gate.

**Content-quality properties:**
- Hard-decision prompts surface the role's substance; filler becomes visible.
- Fixed instructions separated from placeholders; strike/anti-filler cues present.
- A generic one-role form that generalizes across representative cases.

**Discriminating test:** Do the prompts surface the role's hardest decisions and make
omission/filler visible when a real author fills the template?

**Exemplar:** A template whose quality bar is a sharp, role-specific prompt (the role's
discriminating test), whose fixed instructions and placeholders are distinct, and which makes it
obvious when an author strikes content rather than filling it — generalizing across real cases.

**Anti-example:** A template with a vague generic prompt, boilerplate that invites form-filling, or
no way to tell a filled section from a struck one — so an author produces a well-formed empty
record.

## Meta-Question

What does the author of an index or template need that shape validation alone will not tell them?
For an index it is whether the map routes to current authority without silent omission; for a
template it is whether the prompts surface the role's hardest decisions. The template role's own
bar is the model for the rest: prompts, not gates.
